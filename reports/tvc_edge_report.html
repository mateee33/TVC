Where the edge is, and where it isn't

Six candidate edges were tested on five years of Binance spot, perpetual, funding and open-interest data with pre-registered hypotheses, realistic fees, and an out-of-sample period that starts in 2024. Three survived. The bot trades the combination: a funding-filtered cash-and-carry core with two small long overlays that buy forced selling.

The three edges

core · market neutral

Funding-filtered cash-and-carry

long spot 1x + short perp 1x while trailing-7d annualised funding > 10%; exit when < 0

Perpetual longs pay shorts a funding rate that averaged 12–15% a year on the majors. Holding the hedged pair collects it with only basis risk. The filter keeps the book out of negative-funding stretches.

overlay · long, 4 hours

Liquidation-cascade reversal

1h return ≤ −3σ (vs trailing 30d) and open interest down ≥ 3% in that hour → buy the perp for 4h

A sharp drop with open interest collapsing is forced deleveraging. Those drops revert; equally sharp drops without a fall in open interest mostly do not. Detection must be fast: on hourly bars the effect fades.

overlay · long, 1 hour

Post-settlement rebound

funding just paid > 5 bps per 8h (normalised) → hold the perp long for the hour after settlement

Longs lighten up into an expensive settlement and come back after it. Strong across 47 perps and 12,598 events, but it only fires in hot funding regimes and has had almost no events since 2025.

What did not survive

Robustness of the shipped strategy

Each row changes one thing. The strategy keeps a positive out-of-sample Sharpe under every variation tried, including doubled costs and a one-bar-stale open-interest feed.

Method

Pre-registration. Each study states its hypotheses and a primary specification before results are computed; variants are reported alongside. Where a parameter was searched, the deflated Sharpe ratio accounts for the number of trials.

Costs and funding. Taker fee plus slippage on every unit of turnover; funding is paid or received by the position actually holding at each settlement. Maker and doubled-cost variants are shown.

Statistics. Sharpe on daily returns, Newey–West t-statistics, stationary-bootstrap confidence intervals, probabilistic Sharpe.

Survivorship. The cross-sectional study uses every USDT perpetual that ever listed, delisted ones included.

Timing. Bars are indexed by open time; a position decided at a bar's close earns the next bar. Open interest used at a bar is the snapshot at that bar's close; using a one-bar-stale snapshot changes nothing.

Parity. The backtest above calls the same target_positions function the live bot calls on its rolling 31-day window.

Limitations to keep in view

Five years and one cycle. 405 cascade events is a modest sample, and SOL contributes most of the overlay return; ETH shows no cascade effect at all.

The carry's Sharpe is basis risk only. Counterparty failure, liquidation of the short leg in a violent rally, and delisting basis gaps are not in the data.

The rebound overlay was negative in-sample on these three symbols and has had almost no events since 2025. It stays in because the 47-symbol evidence is strong, and it is one flag to switch off.

Paper mode fills at last price plus slippage. Partial fills, outages and queue position are not simulated. None of this is investment advice.

tvc status

Live orders need exchange API keys in the environment and --live; start on the testnet with --sandbox. Risk limits flatten and halt the bot on a 5% daily loss or a 25% drawdown.
